The idea

An NFT is a token issued by a contract (a small program on a blockchain, here mostly Ethereum). Each token is three things: the ownership record, on the blockchain and permanent; the pointer, the address the contract gives for the token; and the content - the token's record (a small file with its title, traits and links), its image and any animation - living wherever the pointer says. Three things can fail: the content (a company's server closes), the pointer (the issuer changes it) and the keeper (whoever holds the copies stops). So every work gets two jobs, never mixed: keep (hold the bytes ourselves) and prove (show what the work was, and when).

Three keepers

Pinata and Filebase: two IPFS providers. IPFS is a public network that fetches a file by its CID (content identifier), which is computed from the file's own bytes: the same bytes, stored the same way, always give the same CID and any change gives a new one, so a CID is also a fingerprint. To pin a CID is to ask a provider to keep that file online; any IPFS gateway can then serve it. Amazon S3 Deep Archive: the third, cold copy, as CAR files (one file holding an IPFS file or folder with all its pieces, which restores to the same CID), locked against deletion.

Files (IPFS addresses) in our ledger: 29,030 - pinned at Pinata 28,926, pinned at Filebase 29,021, archived in S3 29,021; the few exceptions are known and listed in our records.

The proofs

  • Fingerprints: the SHA-256 of our copy and its CID, for the A3, C and D works, in a public list: 6,375 files of 3,042 works (nft-provenance: the November 2026 list, its two addenda, and their timestamp proofs).
  • Wayback witnesses: dated captures by the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine of the work as it was served (the item page with a screenshot, the image and the record): 8,170 captures of 8,063 addresses.
  • Our own web archives (WACZ): a second witness, for the 899 works the Wayback Machine had not yet captured when its save service paused on 14 Nov 2026: 20 bundles, each at the three keepers - archived; replay of the OpenSea item page not guaranteed (the bundle list and its timestamp proofs).
  • Bitcoin timestamps (OpenTimestamps): each public list and bundle is anchored in a Bitcoin block, proving it existed by then; anyone can check a proof without trusting us. Each proof was checked against its block at two independent block explorers.

Art Blocks: our copies of the record, image and live view of 356 works, and the code libraries their live views load, each at all three keepers; 356 of these works are in the public fingerprint list; 356 have their pages here.

What a new marketplace would see

A new marketplace knows only the chain: it reads each token's pointer and goes there. A fully on-chain work (A1) shows. A work on IPFS (B) shows as long as anyone still pins its CID - our pins, our restored S3 copies or anyone else's copy, and the CID itself proves the bytes are right; after a pointer change it shows the new content, while the original survives at its CID with our record of it. An Arweave work (A2) shows while Arweave serves it. An Art Blocks work (A3) shows nothing by its pointer, which names Art Blocks' server, but the art can still be redrawn from the chain, and the record and preview would come only from copies like ours. A company-server work (C, D) shows nothing: our copies survive and the proofs show which copy is the work, but someone must connect token and copy - the issuer pointing the contract at our CID, a marketplace or index accepting the evidence, or pages like these.

Copies, witnesses and timestamps preserve and prove a work, but never change what a marketplace shows: marketplaces follow the pointer.
